Worked example: SLO stack for a case-routing agent

100,000 cases per month; the SLI that measures routing, the SLO that contracts it, and the number of failures the budget permits

SLIMeasuresSLOError budget per month
AvailabilityShare of inbound cases the agent completes and accepts99.5% (below the 99.9% platform SLA)500 cases
Acceptance rateShare of routings a supervisor accepts without correction99.8%200 cases
LatencyShare of cases routed within 30 minutes of arrival99% under 30 min1,000 cases

Budget arithmetic: availability 0.5% of 100,000 is 500. Acceptance 0.2% of 100,000 is 200. Latency 1% of 100,000 is 1,000.

A bad prompt that collapses acceptance to 90% is 10,000 bad routings in the month, far over the 200-case budget. The acceptance SLI crosses its threshold, the burn rate goes red, and the runbook's first action is to roll back the prompt and re-run the affected batch with the review gate restored.

The budget, not a vibe, drives the release policy: ship while inside it, halt non-critical changes when the prior four weeks have blown it.
