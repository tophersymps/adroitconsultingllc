The drill proves the plan, or it fails it

A scheduled restore test turns written procedure into measured outcome

Schedule a restore into a sandbox

Quarterly to a Full Sandbox or Developer environment. Restore the critical objects and metadata from the backup service so the test is real, not a smoke check.

Quarterly partial
Time the recovery

Run under a stopwatch. Measure the achieved RTO and compare it to the target. A two hour claim no one has timed is an untested target until a drill confirms it.

Measure RTO
Validate the data loss

Check whether replication covered every critical object and whether data loss stayed inside the RPO. Gaps surface here while the stakes are still a sandbox.

Validate RPO
Learn and update the runbook

Write down what the drill revealed: coverage gaps, restore time overruns, steps only one person understood. Fix the procedure so the next restore is faster.

Lessons learned
Anti-pattern: never drill

A plan that is never tested assumes the documented procedure works under stress. The first restore attempt happens during a real disaster and reveals the gaps.

Untested plan
Anti-pattern: one-person knowledge

Recovery procedure understood by a single expert who documented nothing. When they leave, the runbook cannot be executed by anyone else under pressure.

No knowledge transfer

Annual full recovery drill restores a complete environment. Backup personnel, not just the expert, must be able to execute the runbook.
