High-Volume Event Patterns: Streaming, Pacing, and Platform Event Limits

AC
Adroit ConsultingSeptember 1, 2026 · 11 min read

Lesson 7 taught you the two vehicles on the Salesforce event bus: Platform Events for business intent and Change Data Capture for record state.1 A few hundred thousand an hour is a different animal — the bus is a shared, multitenant resource with hard allocations.2

Streaming is the easy part

Pub/Sub API gives you a durable, replay-capable stream instead of the old CometD dance.4 The design question is not "which transport" but "who is allowed to fall behind, and for how long".

Replay is a budget, not a feature. Every subscriber that reconnects from a stored replay ID consumes the same retention window everyone else is competing for.3

Pacing against rolling limits

Allocations are enforced on a rolling 24-hour window, which means a burst at 23:00 spends tomorrow's capacity.2 Pace at the publisher, not at the subscriber — by the time the subscriber sees the flood, the allocation is already gone.

The architect's job at this scale is to keep the flood from becoming a failure: stream, pace, and know the limits cold.1

Anatomy — why it does not read as part of the exercise

The check panel is the loudest block on the page: navy slab, red-tinted glow, 20px radius, white primary button. The Sources block is the quietest: no card, no shadow, no radius, a 64px navy rule, a mono kicker at 11px. The separation is register, not a divider line — a heavier border between two cards would still read as "another card in the exercise".

The anchor offset — one property

Both footnote targets (sup[id^="fnref-"]) and source rows (li[id^="fn-"]) — plus every lesson heading — carry scroll-margin-top: var(--lesson-anchor-offset). The value is the sticky stack: site header (64) + the audio bar's 24px margin + its card + a 16px gutter.
