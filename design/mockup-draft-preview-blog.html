Serving LLMs on Kubernetes in 2026: A Field Guide for the Rest of Us

LLM Kubernetes Self-Hosting Inference
AI & Infrastructure

Self-hosting an LLM used to be a weekend project for infrastructure masochists. In 2026 it is a budget question — and the answer is usually Kubernetes, whether you like it or not. This field guide covers the parts nobody blogs about: the cold-start tax, the KV-cache budget, and why your autoscaler will lie to you.

The cold-start tax is real

A warm model answers in 300ms. A cold one takes four minutes to load weights before it answers at all. Every autoscaler in the ecosystem assumes cold starts are cheap — they are not. You need warm pools, and they cost money whether or not anyone is asking questions.

"The cheapest place to make an inference stack fast is at the boundary — not in the model." — Adroit infrastructure notes, 2026

Budget the KV cache, not the VRAM

Most teams size nodes by model weights and wonder why requests die at 60% utilization. The hidden consumer is the KV cache: at 32K context, the cache for a 35B MoE model eats more memory than the weights. Size for peak context, not for weights.

  • Pin every model to an exact revision — quantized files drift silently
  • Set a per-deployment max-context and enforce it at the gateway
  • Warm the pool before traffic, not after the pager fires
# example: warm-pool budget for a 35B MoE deployment
apiVersion: inference.k8s.io/v1
kind: WarmPool
metadata:
  name: llm-warm
spec:
  replicas: 2          # keep 2 pods always loaded
  maxContext: 32768    # budget for peak, not average
  preload: /models/qwen-3.6-35b-a3b-q4.gguf

Why your autoscaler lies

Horizontal Pod Autoscaling treats requests like CPU. Inference is a queue, not a load — one long generation saturates a pod while the metrics read zero. You need a queue-depth autoscaler and generous headroom, or your p99 becomes a coin flip.
