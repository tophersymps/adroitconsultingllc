Generative AI arrived in Salesforce as Einstein GPT, embedded where sales teams already work: the record page, the email composer, the call, and the opportunity pipeline. The promise was that the sales rep stops writing and starts approving. The reality is more selective. Some Einstein GPT use cases earn their keep within weeks, while others produce drafts a rep has to rewrite from scratch. The difference is rarely the model. It is whether the use case sits on a clean, well-governed data foundation and whether the output has a workflow that forces a human checkpoint.

This article separates the practical use cases for sales teams from the aspirational ones, gives you the implementation shape of each, and flags the data and security prerequisites that decide whether a rollout succeeds.

## Start with email drafting and sales emails in the flow of work

The first use case that earns its keep is conversational email drafting directly in the Lightning email composer. Einstein generates reply drafts for inbound leads and contacts from the context of the record, and it generates outreach emails with tone and language controls that match how the team actually writes.[^1] The win is not that the drafts are perfect. They are not, and they should not be pretending to be. The win is keystroke elimination: the rep starts from a structurally sound draft instead of a blank composer, trims a few sentences, and sends.

The discipline that matters is treating the generation settings as a first-class deliverable. Set the tone and length controls to match the executive who owns the account, gate the draft behind a mandatory human review, and log which fielded emails were sent versus edited before sending. Teams that skip these three steps end up with identical AI-sounding outreach that prospects can smell, and they lose the personalization advantage that was the whole point.

## Summarize the record so the rep reads the account before the call

The second high-value use case is record summarization. Einstein can generate a summary of the account, the opportunity, and the activity history that gives a rep the state of the deal in seconds rather than a scroll through twenty activity records. Practically, this means the rep walks into a call with the previous conversation, the open objections, and the next committed step already in front of them.

The implementation note is that summary quality tracks data quality directly. If the org has stale contacts, orphaned activities, or duplicate accounts, the summary will confidently repeat all of it. Run your data health pass before you turn on summaries on a large base, or the first impression your reps get is an AI that does not know their accounts either.

## Let AI help on the call, not replace the call

The next tier is conversation intelligence. Einstein captures call recordings and surfaces real-time suggestions, and post-call it generates summaries and call dispositions that save the rep the after-call admin.[^1] This is where generative AI moves from drafting to actively participating in the sales conversation, and it is also where the content needs the most guardrail work.

Use the post-call summary as the discipline anchor: it feeds the opportunity history the next rep will read, so it has to be accurate. Keep the real-time suggestion surface small and opinionated, and explicitly train reps on when to ignore it. A suggestion that fires on every call becomes noise by the third week, and noise from an automated coach is worse than no coach because reps learn to stop listening to it.

## Point at the data that is real so the output is real

Every one of these use cases inherits the same failure mode: Einstein GPT generates from the records and objects you expose to it, so the quality ceiling is set by your data model and your permission model.[^2] If a field the model needs is missing, or the sharing rules bury the relevant record, the AI cannot compensate with charm. It will either omit the fact or invent a plausible replacement, and a confident hallucination about an account is a legal and reputational liability, not a minor typo.

Set up your grounded context deliberately. Einstein can draw on your org data plus external data configured as sources, so decide explicitly what the model is allowed to see: which objects, which fields, which security controls govern the running user, and what happens when the source data is incomplete.[^2] Treat the generated output the same way you treat anything a less-experienced member of the team drafts: reviewed, edited, and approved by the person accountable for the account.

## The use cases to defer

The use cases worth deferring are the ones that promise to speak for you at scale without supervision: fully automated sequence senders that run the entire cadence without a human click, and AI that generates large-volume personalized assets and sends them directly. Not because the technology cannot produce them, but because the blast radius of an uncaught error compounds with volume. One hallucinated figure in a human-reviewed draft is caught. The same figure in a 5,000-recipient automated send is an incident.

Run those experiments in pilots with hard caps on volume and a mandatory review loop, and promote them only when the human-checkpoint pass rate clears the bar you set for other automated marketing.

## Track lift in the conversation, not just the inbox

Finally, measure the use cases against the metrics that matter to a pipeline. Email drafting and post-call summaries should move reply rates, time-to-first-response, and rep admin time. Run a controlled comparison where one segment gets Einstein-assisted drafting and a matched segment does not, and let the wired-in analytics on your email and call data tell you whether the lift is real. The goal is not to prove the tool works; it is to decide where to invest the next quarter of enablement budget.

## Key takeaways

- Start with the use cases that remove keystrokes and admin: email drafting, record summaries, and post-call call summaries.
- Quality follows data and permissions, not model size. Govern the records Einstein can see before you expect accurate output.
- Keep a human review checkpoint on every generated draft, especially anything that leaves the org.
- Defer fully automated volume sends until your human-review pass rate clears a defined bar.
- Measure controlled lift in reply rates, response time, and rep admin time before you scale the rollout.

Einstein GPT is not a replacement for the sales team. It is an amplifier for the parts of the job that are repetitive, and it is only as good as the workflow and data you put around it. Lead with drafts, summaries, and calls, gate them with review, and the tool becomes a quiet productivity compounder rather than a noisy new inbox monitor.
