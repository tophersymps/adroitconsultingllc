# Lesson 55: Reading Agent Analytics: The Metrics That Matter

An executive asked a delivery lead whether the new agent was working, and the lead answered that deflection was up. It was a true sentence and a useless one, because deflection had risen mostly on a low-volume intent while the agent had quietly gotten worse on the high-volume one everyone cared about. The aggregate number moved in the right direction and the experience moved in the wrong one, and the lead had read the headline without reading the dimension underneath it.

Agent Analytics is built to prevent exactly that mistake, but only if you know what each number measures and where it comes from. The dashboards carry several families of metric, and they are not interchangeable: some count outcomes, some score the answer, some watch stability, and some report how faithfully the agent stayed inside its instructions. Reading the right family for the question is the whole skill, because a moving number only means something once you know which question it answers.

This lesson serves requirement go-2a, "Explain agent analytics and agent optimization.", in the Governance and Observability domain at ten percent of the exam, and it is material the Agentblazer prep trails do not cover in a dedicated module. It takes apart the dimensions an operator reads, the exact definitions of the effectiveness rates, the Session Outcome classification that sits over them, the Quality Scores that come from a separate evaluator, the supporting usage, health, and trust dimensions, and the Performance Insights view that turns a trend into a lead. After it, you can name what a metric measures, where it is produced, and which dimension to open next.

## Deep Dive

### Analytics reads trend across many sessions, not one conversation

Agent Analytics is the aggregate half of observability. You reach it from Agentforce Studio under Observe and Optimize, then Analytics, and it aggregates performance across all sessions and presents trends in dashboards powered by Tableau Next.[^1] The dashboards are organized by dimension, and the tabs you get vary by agent type, so a Service Agent and an Employee Agent do not show the same set of dimensions.[^2]

The dimensions to know are Overview, Effectiveness, Usage, Quality Scores, Health, Trust, Voice, User Satisfaction, and Performance Insights.[^2] Each answers a different question. Effectiveness asks whether the agent is completing its job and handling volume. Usage asks how much of the population is engaging. Quality Scores ask whether the answers were good and correctly grounded. Health asks whether the agent is fast and stable. Trust asks whether it stayed inside its instructions and content bounds. Reading them together is reading the health of the deployment.

The rule that governs all of it is that Analytics is for trend across many sessions, never for a single conversation. If you want to understand one interaction, you leave Analytics and open the session view in Agent Optimization, where the individual conversation lives.[^3] Reaching for an aggregate tab to explain one customer's bad experience is the first habit to break, because a rate moved by a thousand sessions will not tell you why one of them went wrong.

Scope is the other half of reading a trend honestly. The filter bar lets you narrow a dashboard by agent, timeframe, channel, and modality, so a rate is always a rate over a defined slice.[^1] A Deflection Rate for voice over the last seven days and the same rate for messaging over a quarter are not comparable numbers, which is why you state the scope before you quote the trend.

### Effectiveness is the outcome dimension, and its definitions are exact

Effectiveness is the outcome dimension, it is the one most exam questions lean on, and its definitions are worth learning to the word. Deflection Rate is the share of sessions that ended without escalation to a support representative or another agent. Escalation Rate is the share that escalated. Abandonment Rate is the share that timed out or ended without engagement, and the default session timeout behind it is two hours.[^2]

The rest of the family is equally precise. Engagement Rate counts sessions where the user received a reply tied to an agent-triggered action, and Salesforce is explicit that a session counts as engaged only when at least one action was triggered, so a friendly chat where no action fired is not engaged.[^2] Success Rate is the share of interactions with action steps that completed without errors. Task Resolution Rate is the share of sessions evaluated as fully resolved by the Trust Layer Task Resolution detector, which makes it a judgment about whether the user's goal was met rather than merely whether a reply was sent.[^4]

Read the family as two groups. Deflection, Escalation, and Abandonment describe where the session ended. Engagement, Success, and Task Resolution describe what happened inside it: whether an action fired, whether that action worked, and whether the task was actually resolved. A scenario that asks whether the agent got the job done is asking for the second group, and a scenario that asks how sessions ended is asking for the first.

### Session Outcome is the classification layer over the rates

Session Outcome is the classification layer that sits over those rates. The stacked outcome chart groups sessions as Deflected, meaning the session ended at the user's request; Escalated, meaning it transferred to a representative or another agent; Abandoned, meaning it timed out or dropped; or Ambiguous, meaning it could not be classified clearly.[^2] The stack is how you see the mix behind a rate, since a falling Deflection Rate could be rising escalation or rising abandonment and the stack tells you which.

From Summer '26 the Outcome uses model-based scoring rather than a bare rule. At session close the platform evaluates a Deflection Score on a scale of one to five that rates whether the user's primary goals were achieved, and an Abandonment Score that is TRUE, FALSE, or UNSURE for whether the user disengaged out of dissatisfaction or an unresolved issue. Each score carries a reasoner that explains how the outcome was reached, visible when you drill into the session.[^2]

That reasoner is why the classification stays honest. When an outcome looks wrong, the explanation usually states plainly what the scorer saw, so the first move is to read the reasoner rather than to distrust the number. And UNSURE is meaningful on its own: it is the scorer saying it could not tell, which often means the session itself was genuinely ambiguous rather than that the scoring failed.

### Quality Scores measure the answer, from a different pipeline

Quality Scores measure the answer rather than the outcome, and they come from a different pipeline, which is why they move independently of the effectiveness rates. They require Audit and Feedback plus Knowledge/RAG Quality Data and Metrics to be turned on, and they are produced by an external model evaluator rather than by the session classifier.[^2]

The scores themselves are precise. Quality Score is a rating from one to five of how relevant the response is to the request. Answer Faithfulness, Answer Relevance, and Context Relevance are each scored from zero to one. Faithfulness is how closely the answer adheres to the grounded source, relevance is how well it answers the question asked, and context relevance is whether the agent retrieved the correct source at all.[^2]

Read them together and the diagnosis appears. A low Context Relevance with a high Answer Relevance means the answer was fluent but grounded on the wrong document, which is a retrieval problem rather than a wording problem, and the fix belongs in the knowledge source or the retriever, not in the prompt.[^2] This is the single most useful reading in the dimension, because it separates a fluent wrong answer from a clumsy right one, and the two demand opposite fixes.

### Usage, Health, and Trust are the supporting dimensions

Usage, Health, and Trust are the supporting dimensions, and each answers a question the outcome and quality dimensions cannot. Usage counts Unique Sessions, Unique Interactions, Unique Users, and Average Interactions Per Session, which is how you read adoption and reach rather than quality, so a Usage number rising does not mean the agent improved, only that more people used it.[^2]

Health watches Agent Interaction Latency, Session Duration, and Error Rate, the operational stability signals that tell you whether the agent is fast and reliable regardless of whether its answers are good.[^2] These are the same three signals the alerting layer monitors, so a Health trend in Analytics and an Agent Health alert are two readings of the same underlying data. Trust surfaces Adherence Response Rate and Average Agent Toxicity Score, which come from the Trust Layer and answer whether the agent is following its instructions and staying inside acceptable content bounds.[^2]

Two smaller dimensions round out the set. Voice adds Interruption Rate for voice-enabled agents. User Satisfaction shows thumbs up and down counts, and it is available for Employee Agents.[^2] Both are narrower than the core dimensions and apply only to certain agent types, which is the reason the tab set differs between a Service Agent and an Employee Agent. Custom scorers you built in Testing Center also surface in Agent Analytics after deployment, so a criterion you gated the release on can keep being measured on live traffic.[^5]

### Performance Insights is the bridge from a number to a cause

Performance Insights is where the aggregate meets the cause, and it is the dimension the exam expects you to name for drilling down. It gives breakdowns of any metric by subagent, intent, or action, so you can take a trend from a dimension tab and narrow it to the specific component driving it before you open a single session.[^1] The flow of a diagnosis runs from the dimension to the breakdown to the session.

That flow is the answer to the executive question from the opening scene. A Deflection Rate that rose overall can fall on the intent that carries the most volume, and Performance Insights is where you separate the two, breaking the rate down by intent until the component that moved is visible. Only then do you go to a session, because reading transcripts without a breakdown is how you accept the first plausible story instead of the true one.

One boundary to state plainly: do not try to diagnose from the Overview tab. Overview is a health check across all dimensions, a set of summary cards for a quick read of the whole deployment, and it is not a root-cause view.[^2]

A practical rule follows from that: let the dimension tab tell you that something moved, and let Performance Insights tell you what to fix. The tab is the smoke, the breakdown is the address, and the session is the door you finally knock on. Skipping the breakdown and going straight to sessions is why so many teams diagnose the loudest complaint instead of the largest cause. The root-cause work happens in Performance Insights and in the session view, and treating Overview as a diagnostic surface is the mistake a busy operator makes on a bad day.

### Worked example: reading a deflection dip without misreading it

Suppose the Effectiveness tab shows Deflection Rate down three points against the prior period. Before reading anything else, you note which dimension moved: Effectiveness, the outcome dimension, so the question is where sessions are ending. You open the Session Outcome stack and see Escalated rising while Abandoned holds flat, which localizes the change to handoffs rather than drop-offs.

You then open Performance Insights and break Escalation Rate down by intent. If the rise is concentrated in a single intent, say a returns intent, you have a lead without reading a transcript. If instead every intent moved together, you suspect something global, such as an instruction change or a knowledge source that shifted. Either way the aggregate has done its job, and only now do you open the sessions the breakdown points at, which is exactly the sequence the next lesson, on diagnosing a regression, builds on.

## Exam Traps

Three misconceptions carry the marks here. The first is believing Engagement Rate counts anyone who talked to the agent: a session is engaged only when the user received a reply tied to an agent-triggered action, so a conversation where no action fired is not engaged, and an answer that equates chatting with engaging has the definition wrong. The second is believing Quality Score comes from the session outcome: outcome metrics come from session data, while Quality Scores come from an external model evaluator and require Audit and Feedback and retrieval quality data to be turned on, so the two families move independently and a clean outcome rate says nothing about answer quality. The third is believing every agent type shows the same tabs: the dashboard dimensions vary by agent type, and some, such as User Satisfaction and Voice, apply only to certain agent types, so a scenario that assumes a fixed tab set is missing that the surface adapts to the agent.

## What a consultant should walk away with

Read Analytics for trend across many sessions and the session view for one conversation, and never trade one for the other. Learn the effectiveness definitions exactly, since Deflection, Escalation, and Abandonment describe where sessions ended while Engagement needs a triggered action, Success needs an action that completed, and Task Resolution is the Trust Layer's judgment that the goal was met. Read the Session Outcome stack to see the mix behind a rate, and read the reasoner when a classification looks wrong. Keep Quality Scores separate from outcomes, since they come from an external evaluator, and read Faithfulness, Answer Relevance, and Context Relevance together to tell a fluent wrong answer from a clumsy right one. Treat Usage, Health, and Trust as the supporting dimensions, respect that the tab set varies by agent type, and always take a moving number into Performance Insights to find the subagent, intent, or action behind it before you open a session.
