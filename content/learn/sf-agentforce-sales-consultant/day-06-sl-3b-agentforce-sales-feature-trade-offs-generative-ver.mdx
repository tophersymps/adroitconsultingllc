# Lesson 6: Agentforce Sales feature trade-offs: generative versus predictive AI and wrong-fit failures

A VP drops a one line request into the steering committee notes: make our reps smarter about which deals to chase. You have standard Sales Cloud, an Inbox licence nobody has connected, and a Sales Engagement entitlement sitting unused since the last administrator left. Everyone in the room has a different idea of what "smarter" means, and the fastest way to fail the project is to turn on every feature the org owns and call it an AI initiative.

The naive answer is to treat AI as one setting. Teams configure Einstein, switch on cadences, and wire up territory management because all three came in the bundle, then wonder why reps ignore the result. Generative features write sentences. Predictive features return numbers. Cadences drive activity. Inbox mirrors a mailbox. These are four different mechanisms solving four different problems, and enabling the wrong one does not just waste money, it produces an answer nobody trusts. A rep who is handed an email draft when they asked for a ranked pipeline stops believing the next request too.

This lesson serves objective sl-3b in the Sales Lifecycle domain, which carries 20% of the exam weight, and asks you to identify when to use Agentforce Sales features and related products such as Sales Engagement, Salesforce Inbox, Enterprise Territory Management, Opportunity Forecasting, Agentforce Sales Einstein, and generative versus predictive AI. By the end you will be able to read a scenario, name the output the business is actually asking for, and pick the feature that produces it, or explain in one sentence why the org does not own the licence to get there.

<TLDR>

- Read the requirement by its output: a ranking number is predictive scoring, a paragraph of language is generative with grounding
- A step sequence is Sales Engagement and a mailbox log is Inbox, so read the verb before the product label
- Check three gates in order: licence owned, permission sets assigned, and whether the question needs the heavier mechanism

</TLDR>


## Deep Dive

### Separate the two AI families before you choose anything

Predictive AI in Agentforce Sales means the Einstein scoring models, and the two you meet most are Lead Scoring and Einstein Opportunity Scoring.[^1] A predictive model ranks records by likelihood to convert or to close. It outputs a score with the top contributing factors, both positive and negative, so a rep can hover over an opportunity and see why Einstein thinks the deal is likely to land. Those models train on your own org's historical data, which is the detail that decides whether they work at all. Feed a model a few dozen closed deals and you get a low confidence result, because there is not enough history to learn from. The model also refreshes on a schedule rather than instantly, and it sits behind a licence you have to own, which for scoring is Sales Cloud Einstein or the Sales Analytics entitlement depending on how the org bought it.

Generative AI means the Agentforce features that produce language. Email drafts, call summaries, and grounded question and answer over CRM data all fall here. The output is a paragraph, a summary, or a reply, and the engine that makes it safe is the Einstein Trust Layer.[^2] The Trust Layer handles secure data retrieval so a prompt is grounded only in records the requesting user can see, data masking so sensitive values are stripped before a prompt leaves Salesforce, zero data retention with the external model providers, and an audit trail of prompts and responses. The distinction matters because the two families fail in completely different ways. A predictive model fails by being wrong about the odds. A generative feature fails by being fluent about the wrong record.

### The verb in the requirement decides the family

You do not need a deep technical read to place a scenario in the right bucket. Read the verb. When the requirement says draft, summarise, compose, or reply, it wants generative output, and it needs grounding. A request to draft a follow-up email that references the open opportunity and the last logged call is generative, and the opportunity and the activity are the grounding the model must be pointed at, with the Trust Layer enforcing that the requesting rep can see those records in the first place. When the requirement says rank, score, prioritise, or predict the likelihood, it wants predictive output, and only the Einstein scoring models return that number.

This is the single most useful habit for the sl-3b scenarios. Take the VP line from the opening scene, make our reps smarter about which deals to chase. That is a ranking. Forty open opportunities, and the rep wants to know which ten to work today. Einstein Opportunity Scoring answers it with a 1 to 99 score and the factors behind it. Now change the requirement to ask for a personalised recap email to each of those forty accounts before a renewal call, and you have crossed into generative territory, and you will want Agentforce drafting grounded in each account's record rather than a scoring model. Same account list, two different tools, and the verb told you which.

There is a trap in the middle. Generative features look impressive, so scenario writers love to offer them for a numeric need. "Which capability should you use to help reps sort their pipeline by likelihood to close?" will list generative email drafting as the first plausible sounding option. It is wrong, because a paragraph is not a ranking. If you keep asking what the output is, a number or a sentence, you stop being fooled by the fluency of the wrong answer.

### Cadence versus Inbox is the most common wrong fit

Sales Engagement and Salesforce Inbox sit in the same corner of the product and solve opposite problems. A cadence is a managed sequence. You define a target list, the step timing, and the actions in order, and the cadence drives the target through those steps, moving from a main track to positive or negative branches based on whether the prospect opens an email or takes a call. The step lands in the rep's Work Queue and To Do List automatically, so the sequence, not the rep's memory, governs what happens next.[^3] That makes cadences the right tool for outbound prospecting, where volume and consistency matter and letting each rep decide the next touch point produces an inconsistent pipeline.

Inbox is mailbox centric.[^4] It does not impose a sequence and it does not own the order of actions. It augments the rep's own email and calendar with logging, tracking, scheduling, and meeting link insertion, so every message can be captured against the right record and every meeting can carry a booking link straight from the rep's availability. Choose Inbox when the requirement is to log every Outlook or Gmail message and event to Salesforce with open and click tracking, without a fixed step sequence. Choose a cadence when the requirement is to work a named prospect list through a defined set of steps and know exactly which touch point each target is on. If someone asks you to use Inbox to enforce a multi-step outbound process, refuse, because Inbox has no concept of a sequence to enforce.

### Enterprise Territory Management is a heavy answer that has to be earned

Enterprise Territory Management, now sold under the Sales Territories name, gives you a custom territory hierarchy that stands apart from the role hierarchy.[^5] You define territory types, build the hierarchy, assign accounts with territory assignment rules, and build a territory model in versions that you must activate before the rules take effect. This is a real build, not a checkbox, and that is why the exam probes it.

The mechanism that decides whether you need it is account access. Whether territories actually drive access to account records depends on whether account sharing with territory is enabled. Even when it is, territories add a parallel access path alongside the role hierarchy, they do not replace it, so roles still control most record access and default sharing. Two orgs that look almost identical split on a single question: does account ownership need to diverge from who has access, and does the sales model follow named territories rather than managers? If both answers are no, you use roles and you do not build territories, because ETM when the only requirement is a reporting segmentation is over-engineering. You can group reports by region without a territory model.

### Forecasting trade-offs live in setup, not in the report

Forecasts look like a reporting feature and behave like a configuration feature. The number that rolls up depends on three setup decisions: the forecast hierarchy, the forecast type, and whether each opportunity's forecast category is populated and whether reps are allowed to override it.[^6] If the requirement is that the forecast equal the plain sum of opportunities, you need the right forecast type, because the type defines the object the forecast is based on, whether it measures revenue or quantity, and the date it measures by. Get the type wrong and the total will not match anything the finance team can reconcile.

Manager override is its own decision. Collaborative Forecasts supports judgments and adjustments, where a manager records confidence in a deal or replaces the expected value, and those adjustments lock once the forecast period closes. If the requirement names manager override, you are not choosing a standard forecast, you are choosing Collaborative Forecasts with adjustment enabled. Territory based forecasting is the last piece, and it has a hard dependency: without Enterprise Territory Management you forecast by role, because the forecast hierarchy is built from the role hierarchy. A territory forecast requirement and an Enterprise Territory Management requirement are therefore the same requirement wearing two hats, and the exam will test whether you see that.

### Licence framing and the cross feature failure modes

Licence framing decides many of these scenarios, and naming it out loud is often the difference between two otherwise plausible choices. Einstein scoring and Sales Engagement are add-ons that consume specific licences, not standard Sales Cloud entitlements. When a requirement is written against an org that only holds standard Sales Cloud, the correct answer either routes to the feature the licence covers or flags the gap and escalates for the entitlement. An option that quietly assumes a licence the org does not own is wrong even if the feature would solve the problem.

The exam also builds distractors from cross feature failures, so name them on demand. Enabling Sales Engagement without assigning the permission sets leaves reps with no Work Queue, because the feature does nothing until the Sales Engagement user and cadence creator permission sets are in place. Turning on a prediction without enough historical records produces a low confidence model, not a useful ranking. Choosing Inbox to enforce a process fails because Inbox has no sequence. Choosing Enterprise Territory Management when the requirement is only reporting segmentation is over-engineering that adds a build nobody asked for. Each of these is a scenario item waiting to happen, and the fix in every case is the same discipline: check the licence, check the permission sets, check the data volume, and confirm the requirement actually needs the heavier mechanism.

### The configuration path

Walk the org before you recommend a feature, because half of these decisions are already made by what is enabled and assigned. Start in Setup under Company Information and Feature Settings, and confirm which Sales add-ons the org actually licences, Sales Engagement, Einstein for Sales, and Inbox, before you name any of them in a design. Open Permission Sets and check the Sales Engagement User, Sales Engagement Cadence Creator, and Inbox permission sets, because the features are inert until they are assigned and a design that skips the assignment step will fail at go-live.

Under Einstein for Sales, open Opportunity Scoring and confirm the model has enough historical closed-won and closed-lost records and is not stuck reporting insufficient data. Open Forecast Settings and the Forecast Hierarchy and confirm whether the org forecasts by role or, with ETM enabled, by territory. To decide cadence versus Inbox, review each rep's mailbox connection under Inbox Settings and the active cadences under Sales Engagement, then match the requirement to sequence or mailbox. Finally, open Object Manager on Account and check whether territory sharing is enabled before you claim that territories drive record access.

## Exam Traps

<Callout kind="warning">

Three traps dominate this objective. The first is assuming generative AI can produce a propensity score. Generative features draft or summarise language, and only the predictive scoring models return that ranking number, so any answer that reaches for a generative capability to order a pipeline is wrong on the mechanism, not just on the label. The second is believing a cadence enforces data quality. Cadences drive activity through a sequence, but they do not require field completion and they do not restrict which opportunities exist, so a cadence cannot be the answer to a data entry problem. The third is thinking Enterprise Territory Management replaces the role hierarchy. ETM adds a territory hierarchy for account assignment and for territory based forecasting, while roles still control record access and default sharing, so an answer that says territories replace roles has the architecture backwards.

Beyond the three, watch for the licence trap and the permission trap. An option that assumes Sales Cloud Einstein or Sales Engagement on a standard Sales Cloud org is invalid no matter how well it fits the business problem, and an option that enables cadences without assigning the permission sets leaves reps with an empty Work Queue. Both are the same exam habit: confirm what the org owns and what it has assigned before you confirm what the feature does.

</Callout>


## What a consultant should walk away with

<KeyTakeaways>

- Read the requirement by its output: a ranking number means predictive scoring and a paragraph of language means generative grounded through the Trust Layer
- A step sequence means Sales Engagement and a mailbox log means Inbox, while a cadence drives activity rather than data quality
- Check three gates in order: does the org own the licence, are the permission sets assigned, and does the question need the heavier mechanism
- Territory forecasting is a territory management requirement in disguise, and manager override locks once the forecast period closes

</KeyTakeaways>
