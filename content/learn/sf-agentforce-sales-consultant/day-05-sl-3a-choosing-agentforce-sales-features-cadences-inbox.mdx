# Lesson 5: Choosing Agentforce Sales features: cadences, inbox, territories, forecasting, and Einstein

A team asks for one thing: help reps work a sequence of calls and emails, with a prioritized list of who to call each morning. You deliver a beautifully configured forecast type with product family roll-ups. The reps still have no call list, and the manager still cannot see whether the sequence was worked. You built a revenue feature for an activity problem. The requirement named a sequence; you answered with a number.

This is the failure this objective is built to catch. Agentforce Sales is an umbrella over several products, and each one owns a different part of the selling day: sequences belong to Sales Engagement, mailbox logging to Inbox, account ownership to territories, revenue roll-ups to forecasting, and propensity scores to predictive Einstein. When a requirement names one of those jobs and you answer with a product that owns a different job, the configuration can be perfect and still score zero.

This lesson serves requirement sl-3a in the Sales Lifecycle domain, at 20 percent of the exam. You will learn what each Agentforce Sales product actually does, and a selection rule based on the requirement's verb that lets you route any scenario to the right one.

<TLDR>

- Agentforce Sales is an umbrella of products, and each owns a different part of the selling day
- Route a requirement to the product that produces the output the business asked for
- Read the requirement's verb: sequence, mailbox logging, account ownership, revenue roll-up, a propensity number, or language

</TLDR>


## Deep Dive

### One umbrella, several products

Agentforce Sales is an umbrella over several distinct products, and each maps to a different part of the selling day. Sales Engagement is the cadence engine, running a defined sequence of steps against a target list. Salesforce Inbox is the email and calendar layer that works with the rep's existing mailbox. Enterprise Territory Management is a coverage model built on its own territory hierarchy. Opportunity Forecasting is the revenue roll-up engine that projects against quota. Agentforce Sales Einstein spans two families, the predictive features that return scores and the generative features that produce language.

The exam asks you to route a stated requirement to the right one, and the routing works because each product owns a distinct output. A cadence produces touches and tasks. Inbox produces logged and tracked email. Territories produce account ownership. Forecasting produces a roll-up number. Predictive Einstein produces a propensity score. Generative Einstein produces text. Name the output the requirement wants, and the product is whatever produces that output.

A quick routing drill makes it concrete. Reps cannot tell who to call next: Sales Engagement. Email is not showing on the record: Inbox. Account ownership does not match how the business actually sells: Enterprise Territory Management. The forecast number does not match the pipeline: Opportunity Forecasting. Reps do not know which deals to work first: predictive Einstein. A rep wants a first draft of a follow-up: generative Einstein. Six requirements, six products, and the only thing that changed was the output the business asked for.

### Sales Engagement is the cadence engine

Sales Engagement, formerly High Velocity Sales, is the cadence engine. A rep works a defined sequence of steps, such as a call, an email, a LinkedIn touch, or a custom step, against a target list, and the work queue surfaces who to touch next and when [^1]. The cadence creates the tasks that drive the sequence, so a manager gets visibility into whether the steps were worked. Choose it when the requirement reads as reps needing a repeatable multi-touch sequence with reminders and a prioritized call list.

Be precise about what it is not. It is not a forecasting tool, it does not score deals, and it is not autonomous. A cadence follows fixed steps and a human works each one, so a requirement for autonomous outreach is a different feature. When you see a stem about consistent follow-up that a manager cannot observe, Sales Engagement is the answer because it is the only listed product whose job is the sequence.

The implementation detail worth keeping is the license and the work queue. Cadences need the relevant add-on before they run, and the work queue is what turns a sequence into a daily call list, so a build that adds cadences without assigning a work queue leaves the prioritized list empty [^1]. That is the gap a scenario probes when it says reps were placed in a cadence but never saw a next step.

### Salesforce Inbox works the mailbox

Salesforce Inbox is the email and calendar layer that lives inside Outlook or Gmail and on mobile. It does the thing a cadence does not: it works with the rep's existing mailbox rather than imposing a sequence, adding email logging to Salesforce, open tracking, send later, and meeting scheduling links. Pick Inbox when the requirement is that reps live in Gmail and need their emails logged to Salesforce automatically, with tracking and scheduling built in.

The exam splits cadence and Inbox on exactly this line, and it is a sequence-versus-mailbox decision. A cadence changes how the rep works by imposing steps; Inbox changes how the mailbox behaves by logging and tracking what the rep already does. Do not pick Inbox for territory carving or pipeline forecasting, and do not pick a cadence for a rep who simply wants their Gmail to record itself. Read whether the requirement is about the sequence or the mailbox and the pair separates cleanly.

Inbox and Activity Capture together cover the email visibility gap, and neither one is a sequencing tool. If the problem is that a rep's conversations never land on the record, the answer is mailbox logging. If the problem is that the rep does not know what to do next, the answer is a sequence. That single question resolves most cadence-versus-Inbox stems before you get to the options.

### Enterprise Territory Management carves coverage

Enterprise Territory Management is a separate data model built on a custom territory hierarchy that does not use the role hierarchy for roll-ups [^2]. Territory model versions let you plan a future-state carve, preview it, and then activate it, and territory assignment rules place accounts into territories. A model sits in planning until you activate it, and only then does assignment run. Choose it when the requirement is that account ownership or forecasting must follow named territories rather than the manager tree, or that you need to test a new carve before going live.

Two operational details decide feasibility. Mass transfer of accounts between territories is a governance event, and territory sharing settings determine whether users assigned to a territory actually gain the access the design assumes. A scenario that describes accounts reassigned to territories that do not match the role structure, plus a request to test a new carve before activation, is describing Enterprise Territory Management almost word for word, because the model version and the preview step exist for exactly that.

The reason model versions exist is worth stating plainly. A carve is a hypothesis about how the business should be covered, and the business will want to see account counts and forecast impact before committing. Planning a version, previewing the assignments, and activating only what holds is the workflow that lets you change coverage without a live disruption. When a scenario says the org wants to test a new territory structure before it takes effect, that is the model version feature described in plain terms.

### Opportunity Forecasting predicts revenue against quota

Opportunity Forecasting, delivered through Collaborative Forecasts, is about predicting revenue rather than closing deals. Forecast categories such as Pipeline, Best Case, Commit, Closed, and Omitted come from the opportunity's forecast category field, which maps from its stage, and the amounts roll up the forecast hierarchy and can be snapshotted into forecast history for point-in-time comparison [^3]. Forecast types let you roll up by revenue, quantity, or product family, and quotas attach to forecast managers per period [^4].

Choose it when the requirement is that managers need a weekly roll-up of commit versus best case against quota. Keep the scope tight in your head: forecasting answers how much revenue is expected from the pipeline in a period. A requirement to see which specific deals a rep is working is a report, not a forecast, because forecasting aggregates by category and period while a report lists the underlying records.

One nuance to file away: forecast categories come from the opportunity's forecast category field, which defaults from the stage, so the same pipeline can read as different numbers depending on where the stage-to-category mapping sits [^3]. That is why forecasting is a configuration project rather than a report you bolt on, and why a scenario about a number that looks wrong often resolves to a mapping rather than to the deals themselves.

### Einstein spans predictive and generative

Agentforce Sales Einstein spans two families you must keep apart. The predictive features, Einstein Lead Scoring and Einstein Opportunity Scoring, train on your org's history and return a numeric propensity. Opportunity scoring gives each open deal a score from 1 to 99 with the top contributing factors, and it refreshes on a schedule as the model is reanalyzed [^6]. Lead scoring ranks leads by how much they resemble leads that converted before [^5]. A question that needs a propensity number wants the predictive model.

The generative features produce language. Agentforce itself, generative email drafts, call summaries, and grounded question answering fall here, and the Einstein Trust Layer governs how data is masked and retained on the way to the model [^7]. A requirement that needs a drafted email or a natural-language answer wants a generative capability. The trap is that both families live under the Einstein and Agentforce banner, so a stem that names the banner tells you nothing; read what the output has to be. A number is predictive, and language is generative.

Keep the data prerequisite in view for the predictive side. Scoring models need enough history to beat a default, and they refresh on a schedule, so a young data set may produce a weak score rather than a strong one. That is a reason to qualify the recommendation, not a reason to switch to a generative feature, because generative capability answers a different question and swapping families to hide a data gap is exactly the wrong-fit failure this objective warns about.

### Read the requirement's verb

The selection rule is to read the requirement's verb, and it resolves almost every scenario in this objective. Sequence means Sales Engagement. Mailbox logging means Inbox. Account ownership model means Enterprise Territory Management. Revenue roll-up against quota means Opportunity Forecasting. A propensity number means predictive Einstein. Drafting or answering in language means generative Einstein.

When a requirement names more than one, the primary verb wins, and the correct answer is the feature that owns that verb. A scenario that says reps need a sequenced cadence and also want a forecast is testing whether you notice the verb that carries the actual ask. Tie each feature to its own output and the distractors stop working, because a wrong option is almost always a real product attached to the wrong verb.

### The build sequence in six steps

<Steps>

<Step title="In Setup">

In Setup, open Feature Settings then Sales to confirm which Sales products the org already has enabled before you plan the design.

</Step>

<Step title="For sequences">

For sequences, open Sales Engagement (High Velocity Sales) Settings and enable Sales Engagement, then assign the Sales Engagement User and Sales Engagement Cadence Creator permission sets.

</Step>

<Step title="For Inbox">

For Inbox, enable or install the Salesforce Inbox package and assign the Inbox permission set to the users whose mailboxes you are connecting.

</Step>

<Step title="For territories">

For territories, open Territory Models, create a model, build the territory hierarchy with Assignment Rules, then activate the model version.

</Step>

<Step title="For forecasting">

For forecasting, open Forecast Settings and turn on forecasts for the chosen forecast type and hierarchy (role, territory, or custom), then set the forecast category and default period.

</Step>

<Step title="For Einstein">

For Einstein, open Opportunity Scoring under Einstein for Sales to build or enable a prediction, and confirm the historical data and user licence requirements first.

</Step>

</Steps>


## Exam Traps

<Callout kind="warning">

Three traps dominate this objective. The first is thinking Enterprise Territory Management rolls up the role hierarchy. ETM has its own territory hierarchy, so an answer that equates territories with the manager tree is wrong, and the correct design reasons about both structures together. The second is believing Sales Engagement cadences forecast revenue. Cadences only drive rep activity, while forecasting happens in Opportunity Forecasting against quota, so a stem that asks for a commit number is not answered by a cadence. The third is treating Einstein Opportunity Scoring and Agentforce drafting as the same capability. Scoring returns a numeric propensity, while Agentforce produces language, so a requirement for a propensity score rules out a generative answer, and a requirement for a drafted reply rules out a score.

</Callout>


## What a consultant should walk away with

<KeyTakeaways>

- Sales Engagement owns touches and a prioritized call list; Inbox owns logging and tracking the rep's existing mailbox
- Enterprise Territory Management owns account ownership that follows a deliberate carve you can preview before activating
- Opportunity Forecasting owns committed and best case revenue rolled up against quota, with forecast types for revenue, quantity, or product family
- Predictive Einstein owns a number that ranks work, and generative Einstein owns language under the Trust Layer; tie each product to its output

</KeyTakeaways>
