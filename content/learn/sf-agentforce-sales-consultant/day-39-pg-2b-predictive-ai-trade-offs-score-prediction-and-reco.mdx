# Lesson 39: Predictive AI Trade-offs: Score, Prediction, and Recommendation, Discovery, and Conversation Insights

A sales VP tells you the forecast is useless because the system keeps flagging deals that everyone knows are fine, and what she actually wants is for reps to be told what to do next on a renewal that is wobbling. You go looking for a bigger score and realize the request was never about a score. She wants a recommendation, and a recommendation is not a number. It is a number plus a rule about who should do what, and it lives in a different part of the platform.

This is the trade-off that shapes the second half of the AI domain. A score and a prediction and a recommendation get used as synonyms in conversation and they are three distinct things, with three distinct prerequisites and three distinct failure modes. Add two more tools, Einstein Discovery for outcomes with no shipped scorer and Einstein Conversation Insights for what was said on a call, and the consultant's real job is scoping: deciding which tool answers the requirement, what data each one consumes, and what breaks when that data is thin.

This lesson serves requirement pg-2b, "Understand what predictive AI tools are available in Agentforce Sales," in the Predictive and Generative AI domain, which carries 13 percent of the exam. Lesson 38 covered the shipped scorers and their thresholds; this lesson covers the rest of the predictive surface and how to choose among the tools. By the end you can separate a score from a prediction from a recommendation, explain what Einstein Discovery builds and where its models live now, describe when Einstein Next Best Action is the answer, name what Conversation Insights captures and the consent it requires, and diagnose the four ways a predictive feature fails.

<TLDR>

- A score ranks records, a prediction estimates a target, a recommendation suggests an action
- Einstein Discovery builds a model for a target with no shipped scorer
- Predictive features fail in four named modes; name the one before proposing a fix

</TLDR>


## Deep Dive

### A score, a prediction and a recommendation are three things

Three terms get mixed together and the exam separates them. A score is a ranking number, such as an opportunity score of 82 out of 99, that orders records against each other. A prediction is a model output for a target, such as the probability that this lead converts or the expected value of this deal, and it can carry a category or a numeric estimate. A recommendation is a suggested next action, produced when a strategy layer turns a prediction into advice, which is what Einstein Next Best Action does with a strategy built in Flow.

Use the question being asked to pick the term. A question about what to do next wants a recommendation. A question about ranking records wants a score. A question about the model's estimate for one record wants a prediction. The word "advice" or "suggested action" points at the strategy layer, and the word "likelihood" or "probability" points at a prediction. When a scenario describes a VP who wants reps told which single action to take on an at-risk renewal, the answer is not a score and not a sync feature. It is a recommendation, and the mechanism that produces it is a Next Best Action strategy.

![Three cards compare a score, a prediction and a recommendation: a score ranks records against each other, such as an opportunity score of 82 out of 99, a prediction estimates a target such as the probability that a lead converts, and a recommendation is suggested next action produced by a strategy layer built in Flow.](</diagrams/day-39-pg-2b-predictive-ai-trade-offs-score-prediction-and-reco/1-three-nouns.png>)

The reason the distinction matters operationally is that a prediction without a strategy layer is just a number sitting on a record. Turning it into advice requires someone to encode the business's judgment about what should happen when the number crosses a threshold, and that encoding happens in a Flow. The strategy is where the business logic lives, and it is the piece that gets skipped when a team assumes the model will recommend for them.

### Einstein Discovery builds a model for a target you choose

Einstein Discovery is the feature that moves past a single shipped score into automated analysis of your own data.[^2] It creates a story that surfaces the drivers of an outcome and builds a predictive model from them, and you can deploy that model back into Salesforce so it writes a prediction field on a record and can trigger a Flow. Underneath the interface it is doing supervised machine learning and statistical analysis, and its documentation presents the full arc from descriptive analysis of what happened, through diagnostic analysis of why, to predictive analysis of what could happen and prescriptive analysis of what you can do about it.[^1]

Two facts about its current state are worth carrying into the exam. First, the naming has moved: Einstein Discovery stories are now called models, and you can expect to see the older term in some places while the documentation catches up. Second, the location has moved. Discovery began inside CRM Analytics, and it now also exists in Data Cloud with models managed through the model surface, which is why a current exam answer names Data Cloud and the model surface rather than a legacy analytics app alone.[^8] The enabling work is the same regardless: a clean dataset with a defined outcome and a place to write the prediction back.

Use Discovery when the prediction you need has no shipped scorer. Renewal likelihood, churn, discount leakage and expansion propensity are all targets Salesforce does not hand you a feature for, and they are exactly the shape of problem Discovery solves. The same feature also gives you the drivers, which matters when a leader says she wants to see what is pushing the number. A scenario that asks for renewal likelihood plus a view of the factors is a Discovery scenario, and the two-part requirement is the tell.

### Einstein Next Best Action is the strategy layer

Einstein Next Best Action is the mechanism that turns a prediction, or a rule, into a recommendation a rep can accept or reject. It works through standard Salesforce records called Recommendations, which are processed by strategies and associated with flows. A strategy determines which recommendation records surface, using business rules, predictive models and other data, and the result is a context-specific suggestion presented on a page.[^3]

The build has a shape worth knowing. You create the action flow that runs when a user accepts the recommendation, because the recommendation is a door and the flow is what is behind it. You create recommendation records, either as records on the Recommendation object or built from other data inside the strategy.[^4] Then you build the strategy, and Salesforce recommends doing that in Flow Builder using the Recommendation Strategy flow type, with Strategy Builder available as the older path. The strategy reads records, filters them, and assigns the survivors to the output recommendations collection, which is the only collection Next Best Action displays. From there you place the strategy on a Lightning record page, an app home page, an Experience Cloud site page, a Visualforce page, or an external site, depending on where the advice needs to appear.

![Pipeline diagram: recommendation records on the Recommendation object are read and filtered by a strategy against business rules and predictive models, the survivors are assigned to the output recommendations collection that Next Best Action alone displays, and the result surfaces on a Lightning record page, an app home page, an Experience Cloud site page, a Visualforce page or an external site. Accepting a recommendation runs the action flow behind it.](</diagrams/day-39-pg-2b-predictive-ai-trade-offs-score-prediction-and-reco/2-nba-pipeline.png>)

The exam-relevant point is that the strategy is what makes the recommendation a recommendation. You can have a perfect prediction and no advice, because nothing encoded the rule that says what to do when the number is low. You can also have advice with no prediction at all, because a strategy can surface a recommendation purely on business rules. The combination, prediction feeding a strategy feeding an action, is what a well-built sales org runs.

### Einstein Conversation Insights captures what was said

Einstein Conversation Insights records and transcribes sales calls from connected meeting tools, then surfaces keywords, talk ratios, competitor mentions and storyline tracking so managers coach from evidence.[^5] Managers and reps can play recordings and see call details including the talk-to-listen ratio, and sales leaders can extract conversation trends and visualize them. The standard insight set includes competitor mentions and product mentions, and there is a set of automatic insights that need no configuration, covering things like pricing, next steps, trending and the longest monologue. You can also define custom insights, grouping keywords into categories and specifying which party said the keyword.

Two properties matter for the exam. It is a source of unstructured signal that the scorers and Discovery do not otherwise receive, which is why a question about capturing what was said on a call points here rather than at Activity Capture. Activity Capture syncs email and calendar metadata and content; Conversation Insights listens to the conversation itself and can read talk ratio, sentiment and competitor mentions out of it. That is a different category of input, and it is why the two features coexist rather than overlap.

The second property is the consent obligation. Recording conversations is legally constrained in many jurisdictions, and the platform does not obtain consent for you. A consultant deploys Conversation Insights only where the recording consent and disclosure process is in place, and the consent step is a prerequisite rather than a cleanup item.[^6] If a scenario places call recording in a region with strict consent rules, the right first step is confirming the consent and disclosure process before enabling the feature for a team, not enabling it and disclosing later.

### Predictive features are only as good as the activity and outcome data behind them

Predictive features inherit the discipline of the CRM underneath them, and that is where Einstein Activity Capture earns its keep. It syncs email and calendar events and matches them to the correct contact, lead, opportunity or account through matching rules, so a mismatch does not just lose one email, it writes engagement onto the wrong record and misleads the model.[^7] Duplicate records split the timeline. Contacts stored with inconsistent email addresses never match at all. An org that turned off calendar tracking for a team hands the model nothing to learn from. Before promising a scoring uplift, confirm that the fields and activity history the model consumes are governed, because a model trained on a fragmented timeline produces a confident ranking of a business that does not exist.

This is also why Activity Capture is more than a rep-facing convenience. It is the signal source for the scorers, so switching it off for a team degrades their scores even though no rep asked for it to be on. The correct framing with a skeptical sales leader is that Activity Capture is data infrastructure for the AI features, and its matching and exclusion rules are a design decision rather than an admin detail.

### The four failure modes, named

The failure modes of predictive features group into four, and naming which one is in play is the fastest way to choose a fix. Data volume failure is where the closed history never reaches the thresholds, so everything runs on the global model or returns nothing at all; the fix is time, data hygiene, or segmentation discipline. Configuration failure is where a renamed stage picklist value is no longer mapped to a stage type, so the win rate calculation is quietly wrong; the fix is remapping the picklist values. Signal failure is where the activities the model relies on are missing because sync was never enabled or was disabled for a team; the fix is reconnecting Activity Capture and cleaning up the matching rules. Interpretation failure is where the business treats a score as a verdict and stops doing the discovery that would have changed the outcome; the fix is procedural, and it is a coaching problem rather than a data problem.

![Four cards pair each failure mode with its own fix: data volume failure runs on the global model until the closed history reaches the thresholds, configuration failure from a renamed stage picklist value makes the win rate quietly wrong, signal failure from sync that was never enabled strips the model of activity data, and interpretation failure is the business treating a score as a verdict.](</diagrams/day-39-pg-2b-predictive-ai-trade-offs-score-prediction-and-reco/3-failure-modes.png>)

The reason to name them rather than lump them together is that the fixes do not overlap. Scaling the data does nothing for a broken stage mapping. Fixing the stage mapping does nothing for a team that disabled calendar sync. And no amount of data or configuration work fixes a sales floor that has stopped asking questions. When a scenario describes a predictive feature underperforming, identify which of the four it is before reaching for a remedy.

### Choosing among the tools, and keeping the clocks straight

Choosing among the predictive tools is a scope question. Use the shipped opportunity and lead scoring when you want a standard ranking with no model building and the thresholds can be met. Use Einstein Discovery when you need a prediction for a target with no shipped scorer, such as churn, renewal likelihood or discount leakage, and you need to see the drivers. Use Model Builder and a custom model when the prediction depends on data that is not in the CRM at all, or must run where a customer's infrastructure or region demands. Then add a recommendation layer on top of any of them when the business needs an action rather than a number.

Keep the clocks straight, because questions turn on them. Opportunity scores update every few hours, the opportunity model is reanalyzed roughly monthly or when settings change, and first scores can take about 48 hours. Lead scores refresh multiple times each day, from a model built on the last three years of closed lead patterns. Activity Capture runs on its own sync schedule and is not instant, with up to 24 hours before newly captured data appears in standard reports. Conversation Insights produces its output after calls are recorded and processed, so it also arrives on a delay rather than a live feed. Any option claiming a feature updates in real time is almost always wrong, and the realistic answer is a number that is hours old, grounded in activity that arrived on a schedule.

The build sequence follows the scoping. In Setup you confirm which predictive features the org is licensed for before promising any of them, since Lead Scoring needs Sales Cloud Einstein while Opportunity Scoring reaches a wider set of users. You open Einstein Activity Capture settings and verify the connected accounts, the matching rules, and which teams have sync enabled, because this is the signal source for every scorer. In CRM Analytics or Data Cloud you open Einstein Discovery, create a model against a dataset with a clearly defined outcome, and read the top drivers it surfaces before building further. You deploy the model so it writes a prediction field on the target object, then reference that field in a Flow to act on the result. You build a Next Best Action strategy in Flow when the business needs a recommended action rather than a number, and expose it on the record page. Finally you add the prediction and score fields to reports and list views and agree a review cadence with sales leaders so model drift is caught rather than discovered at quarter end.

## Exam Traps

<Callout kind="warning">

Three traps dominate this objective. The first is believing a score and a recommendation are the same thing. A score ranks records; a recommendation comes from a strategy layer that turns a prediction into an action. A requirement for advice needs Next Best Action, not a better score, and a scenario about coaching reps on the next step is testing exactly that.

The second trap is believing Activity Capture only affects what a rep sees. It also feeds the models, so switching it off for a team degrades their scores even though no rep asked for it to be on. Activity sharing rules govern visibility to people, but the feature's role as model input is the part that surprises people.

The third trap is believing Einstein Discovery still lives only in CRM Analytics. Its story interface is now the model interface, and it has moved into Data Cloud with models managed through the model surface, so current answers name Data Cloud rather than a legacy analytics app alone.

</Callout>


## What a consultant should walk away with

<KeyTakeaways>

- Separate the three nouns: ranking gets a score, an estimate gets a prediction, and what-should-I-do gets a recommendation from a strategy layer
- Reach for shipped scorers for a standard ranking, Einstein Discovery for a target with no shipped scorer, Model Builder for another model or region
- Add Next Best Action when the output must be an action, treat Conversation Insights as needing consent, and treat Activity Capture as load-bearing
- When a feature underperforms, name which of the four failure modes is in play before proposing a fix, since every feature refreshes on a schedule

</KeyTakeaways>
