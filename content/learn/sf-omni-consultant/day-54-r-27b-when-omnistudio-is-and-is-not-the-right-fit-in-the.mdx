The right-fit test is about the shape of the interaction, not the popularity of the tool. Requirement r-27b asks you to justify and recommend architectural choices, showing how OmniStudio components fit into the overall Salesforce and enterprise system landscape. In the Best Fit Solutions domain, worth 15% of the exam, the questions reward consultants who can classify an interaction by its shape and say no to OmniStudio with reasons. The organizing idea is simple: classify the interaction by its shape before choosing a component. Guided and stateful means OmniStudio, simple read means a component, background or event-driven means Apex and platform events.

## Deep Dive

### The right-fit test is about the shape of the interaction

The right-fit test is about the shape of the interaction, not the popularity of the tool. OmniStudio is the right fit when the user journey is guided, multi-step, and stateful: an application, a quote, a claim, an onboarding flow [^1]. It is the wrong fit when the interaction is a single read, a simple form, or a background process. The architect should classify the interaction by its shape before reaching for a component.

The shape of the interaction is the deciding factor. A guided, multi-step, stateful journey, where the user moves through steps and the system remembers what came before, is an OmniStudio fit. A single read, a simple form, or a background process is not. The architect classifies the interaction first, then chooses the component [^1].

The exam tests this directly. An architect evaluating a guided multi-step claim intake flow that will be used by a small team of adjusters should choose an OmniScript. An OmniScript is purpose-built for guided, stateful journeys and handles data under the user's context, which matches a small-team claim intake. It is not the only way to build a form, so that reason is false. It is not optimized for high volume, so that reason is also false. It does not bypass sharing rules; it runs under them, so the last option is incorrect [^1].

### Volume and concurrency are the first disqualifiers

Volume and concurrency are the first disqualifiers. An OmniScript that thousands of users run simultaneously, or an Integration Procedure that fires on every record change in a high-volume object, can exhaust governor limits and degrade the org [^1]. When the expected volume is high, the architect should prefer platform events, Apex batch, or asynchronous processing and reserve OmniStudio for the interactive, human-paced parts of the process.

The volume test is the first filter. OmniStudio components are user-facing and request-response oriented. They are built for human-paced interaction, not for thousands of simultaneous runs or for firing on every record change. When the expected volume is high, the architect prefers platform events, Apex batch, or asynchronous processing [^1].

The exam tests this directly. A team that needs to move data from an external system into Salesforce as part of a nightly process should use an Apex batch job or platform event driven integration. A scheduled, high-volume data load is a background integration, which belongs to Apex batch or platform events, not to user-facing OmniStudio components. An OmniScript requires a user to run it and is not built for volume. An Integration Procedure is request-response and not designed for scheduled bulk loads. A FlexCard only displays data and does not move it [^1].

### The data movement pattern decides between a Data Mapper and an Integration Procedure

The data movement pattern decides whether a Data Mapper or an Integration Procedure is appropriate. A Data Raptor (load or save) is for moving data between Salesforce and the OmniScript's JSON, and it is the right tool when the transformation is simple [^1]. An Integration Procedure is for server-side orchestration, external calls, and multi-step transformations, and it is the right tool when the work must happen off the browser and be reusable across OmniScripts and FlexCards.

### Reusability is a fit signal

Reusability is a fit signal. If the same data transformation or external call is needed by several OmniScripts, a FlexCard, and possibly a future integration, it belongs in an Integration Procedure that is shared, not duplicated inside each OmniScript [^1]. Duplicated Data Raptors and inline logic are a maintenance liability and a sign that the architect chose the component for convenience rather than fit.

### OmniStudio is not the right fit for headless APIs, scheduled integrations, or event consumers

OmniStudio is not the right fit when the requirement is a headless API, a scheduled integration, or a real-time event consumer. Those belong to Apex, platform events, and external services [^1]. The architect should be explicit that OmniStudio components are user-facing and request-response oriented, and that pushing them into background or event-driven roles is a misuse that will surface as governor-limit failures and unpredictable behavior.

The negative case is part of the recommendation. A defensible architecture names what it deliberately does not use and why [^1]. If the team already has a mature Lightning Web Component library, a new guided flow may be better built there for consistency. ### A worked example: a policy renewal reminder

Put the pieces together with a concrete scenario. An architect is asked to design a solution that reminds policyholders their policy is about to renew. The architect classifies the interaction by its shape before choosing any component.

The first requirement is a nightly job that finds policies expiring in the next thirty days and sends a reminder. The architect classifies this as a background process. It is not a guided interaction, and it runs on a schedule. The architect rejects OmniStudio for this part and uses an Apex batch job or platform event driven integration instead [^1].

The second requirement is a guided flow that lets a policyholder update their contact details before renewal. The architect classifies this as a guided, stateful interaction. It belongs in an OmniScript, which guides the user through the steps and handles data under the user's context [^1].

The third requirement is a read-only summary of the policyholder's coverage displayed on their account record page. The architect classifies this as a simple read. It belongs in a FlexCard with a Data Raptor that reads the coverage directly from Salesforce, the lightest correct fit [^1].

The architect writes a short "not used" section in the design document. It records that OmniStudio was deliberately not used for the nightly reminder, because that is a background process, and that the external system's own API was not duplicated in an Integration Procedure, because that would add a maintenance surface without adding capability [^1].

The result is a defensible architecture. Each requirement is classified by its shape, OmniStudio is used where it fits, and the negative decisions are recorded with reasons.

## Config Walkthrough

The right-fit test maps onto four concrete steps.

1. Run a fit classification on every requirement: label it guided interaction, simple read, background process, or event consumer, and reject OmniStudio for the last two unless a specific reason overrides the classification [^1].

2. Estimate concurrency for each OmniScript and Integration Procedure during design, and flag any component expected to run at high volume for a governor-limit review before build [^1].

3. Inventory shared data transformations and external calls across the solution, and promote any that appear in more than one OmniScript into a single reusable Integration Procedure [^1].

4. Write a short "not used" section in the design document that lists the components the team deliberately avoided and the reason, so the negative decisions are recorded and reviewable [^1].

Test the classification against the business process and confirm each component serves the shape of its interaction before you build.

## Exam Traps

Three traps dominate this requirement, and each is a specific, avoidable mistake.

**Using an Integration Procedure for a simple read that a Data Raptor handles.** The extra server round trip adds latency and a failure point for no benefit, and it makes the simple case harder to test. Use a Data Raptor for a simple read [^1].

**Building the same Data Raptor or transformation inside every OmniScript instead of sharing it.** Duplication means a change to the mapping must be made in several places, and the copies drift apart over time. Promote the shared logic into a single reusable Integration Procedure [^1].

**Pushing an OmniScript into a high-volume or background role because it was the fastest way to build.** The script will hit governor limits and time out under load, and the failure is hard to diagnose because it only appears at scale. Prefer platform events, Apex batch, or asynchronous processing for high volume [^1].

The exam tip that ties these together: classify the interaction by its shape before choosing a component. Guided and stateful means OmniStudio, simple read means a component, background or event-driven means Apex and platform events.
