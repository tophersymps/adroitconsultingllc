# Lesson 4: Predictive AI in Data 360: Model Builder, Bring Your Own Model, and Activating Models

A data science team finishes a churn model on Amazon SageMaker, connects it to Data 360, and wires a Flow that should surface the score on the account record. The model settings save without a single error. The Flow runs on schedule. The score field comes back empty. The team spends a day re-checking the endpoint, the input and output schema, and the Flow logic before someone opens the model record in AI Models and notices the step nobody took: the model was saved, but it was never activated. Saving a predictive model only makes it ready for activation. Until you activate it, none of its predictions are consumable anywhere in Salesforce.

That failure is the spine of this lesson. The requirement behind it is sp-2b: "Articulate Data 360's foundational role in generative and predictive AI, covering predictive AI in Data 360 (Model Builder, Bring Your Own Model, AI Models, and the requirement to activate a model before consuming predictions)." It sits in the Solution Positioning domain, which is 14% of the Salesforce Certified Data 360 Consultant exam, the same domain that covers terminology, business value, and how Data 360 grounds generative AI.

This lesson walks the three paths into predictive AI, the place they all live, the difference between a saved model and an activated one, and how an activated score reaches a real decision through Flow, Apex, agents, and real-time data graphs. The activation gate is the single most common way candidates lose points on this requirement, so it gets the most attention.

## Deep Dive

### Predictive AI and generative AI solve different problems and live in the same place

Predictive AI and generative AI are not competing products. They answer different questions, and in Data 360 they share one home. Predictive AI scores an outcome for an entity: the likelihood a customer churns, the propensity that a prospect buys, the predicted lifetime value of an account. Generative AI produces text and conversation: a drafted reply, a summary, an answer to a question. A consultant picks predictive when the business wants a ranking or a score that drives a decision, and picks generative when the business wants content or an answer. Both consume governed data from Data 360, so both inherit the same unification and the same controls.

The destination is the same tab, and this is where exam questions like to trip people. Both predictive and generative capabilities live in AI Models, which is the former Einstein Studio.[^4] Salesforce rebranded the Data Cloud app to Data 360 and renamed the Einstein Studio tab to AI Models, so during the transition you will still see "Einstein Studio" in older pages and screenshots. When a question asks where a predictive model is created, or where the model builder sits, the answer is AI Models in Data 360. It is not a separate product, not Agentforce, and not a tool inside the CRM app. AI Models is the command center for every model an organization builds, connects, or brings, and it is integrated directly into Data 360, which means it also sits under the Einstein Trust Layer.

Getting the naming right matters more than it sounds. A candidate who answers "Einstein Studio" is describing the same place under its old name, but a candidate who points at a different product entirely has misunderstood the architecture. Say AI Models for the current product, and use Data Cloud only where an official page still does, for example a legacy permission set name.

### Model Builder is the no-code path to predictive AI

Model Builder is the clicks-not-code way to create a predictive model. An admin or business user selects the target variable to predict, chooses the training attributes from unified Data 360 data, picks a model type, and trains the model without writing a line of code or holding a data science degree. In its first release, Model Builder trained a model from a single data model object, so the training data is a harmonized DMO rather than a pile of raw source rows.[^3] You can also start from a preconfigured template, which supplies model details, variables, and a recommended algorithm that you adjust before training.

The output is the point. When the model trains, it returns predictions, and it also returns prescriptions and top predictors. A prediction is the score itself. A prescription is a suggested action or value change the model recommends. Top predictors are the training attributes that had the greatest impact on the outcome, and they are how the model explains itself. A churn model that says "this account is likely to churn" is useful; the same model that names the top predictors behind the score tells the business what to fix. That explanatory layer is why Model Builder output is trusted inside a business conversation and not treated as a black box.

Model Builder is the right answer whenever a scenario says a business user or an admin must create a predictive model with clicks. If the question describes a sales operations analyst who wants to predict which opportunities will close, and the constraint is that no code and no data scientist are involved, the answer is Model Builder. It builds and trains a predictive model with clicks using Data 360 data. The strongest distractor, Bring Your Own Model, connects an already-built external model that still required external data science work to produce, so it does not fit a no-code requirement.

### Bring Your Own Model connects a model trained outside Salesforce

Not every organization wants to rebuild its models inside Salesforce. A data science team may have spent months tuning a fraud model on Amazon SageMaker, a demand model on Google Cloud Vertex AI, or a scoring model on Databricks. Bring Your Own Model, or BYOM, is how those already-trained external models get used with Data 360 data. You register the external endpoint, define how the input and output data is shaped, and connect the model to Data 360.

The mechanism that makes this work without a copy is Zero Copy. BYOM provides direct access to Salesforce data in the external machine learning platform and uses a zero-copy approach, which eliminates the need to duplicate the data. Through zero-copy federation, Data 360 acts as the governed single source of truth and exposes harmonized Customer 360 data for inference at the external endpoint, and the prediction outputs flow back in real time to power Salesforce processes.[^2] Because the model's inference runs where the model lives, the data never has to leave its governed home to be scored.

Here is the part candidates get wrong. BYOM simplifies the connection, but it does not remove the need for data science skills. Creating, training, and deploying the model still requires coding expertise on the external platform; only the connection back to Salesforce is simple. So when a question asks what Bring Your Own Model actually does, the precise answer is that it runs inference against an externally trained model, exposing harmonized Data 360 data without moving it. It does not retrain the model inside Data 360, and it does not eliminate the expertise the external team needed.

### BYOLLM is the generative counterpart

BYOM has a generative twin. Bring Your Own Large Language Model, or BYOLLM, connects an externally hosted large language model so an organization can use a model it already trusts. The external provider list includes options such as OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google's Gemini on Vertex AI. The external model is not uploaded to or hosted in Salesforce; Salesforce reaches it through an API. That matters for governance, because the connection sits under the Einstein Trust Layer, which enforces zero-data retention so the third-party provider neither trains on nor retains your data.

Together, Model Builder, BYOM, and BYOLLM make AI Models the command center for both predictive and generative models in Data 360. An admin can build predictive models with clicks, an enterprise can connect predictive models from external machine learning platforms, and an organization can add generative models from external providers it already uses. In all three cases, the models are managed in one place, and any predictive model that runs consumes data governed by Data 360.

The choice between the three paths comes down to where the model and the expertise already live. If the organization wants to build something new without code, that is Model Builder inside Data 360. If the model was built and trained on an external machine learning platform and should stay there, that is BYOM. If the organization has a large language model endpoint it trusts and wants to reuse it, that is BYOLLM. Naming that decision cleanly is what the exam rewards.

### Activation is the gate that trips up candidates

Saving a model and activating a model are two different states, and this is the heart of the requirement. After saving your predictive model settings, the model is ready for activation. To consume predictions, you must first activate the model.[^1] Read those two sentences again, because every scenario on this objective turns on them. Saving only makes the model ready for activation; predictions are consumable only after the model is activated.

An activated model can be consumed in more places than people expect. Its inferences flow into Flow, Apex, predict jobs, batch data transforms, agents, prompt templates, REST calls, and Salesforce Personalization, among others. That range is why the failure surfaces in so many different ways. The score is missing on a record page, the Flow variable is empty, the agent does not have the signal, the personalization rule never fires. The surface varies; the root cause is the same.

So read the diagnostic scenarios carefully. If a scenario describes predictions that never appear where they are expected, the most likely cause is that the model was saved but never activated, and the correct answer is that the model has not been activated. The tempting wrong answers are usually "the model needs to be retrained" or "the model must be rebuilt in Model Builder." Neither is right, because the model trained fine and the connection works. Retraining or rebuilding is unnecessary, and BYOM outputs can be consumed by Flow and Apex just like any other model's output. The missing step is activation.

### Predictive output becomes powerful when it meets real-time context

A score on its own changes nothing. What turns predictive output into business value is meeting it with real-time context at the moment of decision. Real-time data graphs pre-join a profile with its related data so the profile can be retrieved quickly during an interaction, and models scored on that data feed next best action, next best offer, and Agentforce decisioning inside a live customer conversation. The data graph is the retrieval layer that keeps a real-time decision fast enough to be useful.

This is where a consultant earns trust. The job is not to produce a model; it is to connect the score to an action. A churn score that nobody sees changes nothing, and a propensity score with no place to surface is a science project. Every use case should therefore name where the prediction appears and what the business will do with it: the account record that shows a churn risk, the console that recommends the next step, the Flow that triggers a retention offer, the agent that chooses a resolution path. If you can describe the model, the activation, and the action, you have described a working predictive use case rather than a model in a list.

## Config Walkthrough

Walk the path end to end in five steps.

1. Open AI Models, the former Einstein Studio, from the Data 360 app. This is the tab where every predictive and generative model an organization builds or connects is listed and managed, and it is where both the Model Builder and the Bring Your Own Model entry points live.

2. In Model Builder, choose the target variable to predict, select training attributes from a unified DMO, and pick a model type. Decide the outcome first (churn, conversion, value), then choose the harmonized object and attributes that carry the signal, then choose the algorithm or accept a template's recommendation.

3. Train and evaluate the model, review its quality metrics, then activate the model so its predictions become consumable. Read the accuracy metric and the top predictors before you trust the output, and treat activation as a required, separate action rather than something that happens automatically when you save.

4. For Bring Your Own Model, register the external endpoint, map the input and output schema, and confirm zero-copy access to harmonized Data 360 data. The endpoint request and response formats have to match what Data 360 expects, and the zero-copy path is what lets inference run against governed data without a duplicate copy.

5. Consume a prediction by wiring the activated model into a Flow, a record page, or an agent, then verify the output appears where the use case needs it. Test with a real record, confirm the score or prediction is populated, and trace the value back to the model to prove the whole chain works.

## Exam Traps

Three traps dominate this objective.

First, assuming that saving a model makes its predictions available. This is the trap the requirement was written around. Saving only makes the model ready for activation, and predictions are consumable only after the model is activated. If a scenario shows a model that saves cleanly but produces no predictions anywhere, do not reach for retraining or rebuilding. Reach for activation.

Second, assuming Bring Your Own Model removes the need for data science. The connection to Salesforce is simple, but the external model still has to be built and trained on its home platform. BYOM runs inference against an externally trained model and exposes harmonized Data 360 data without moving it; it does not remove the expertise those external teams needed to produce the model in the first place.

Third, confusing predictive and generative capabilities, or placing Model Builder in a different product. Both predictive and generative live in AI Models, the former Einstein Studio. A candidate who answers that Model Builder is a separate product, or who swaps it for an agent tool, has missed the architecture. The same tab holds the click-built predictive models, the external predictive models connected through BYOM, and the external large language models connected through BYOLLM.

## Decision rules to carry into the exam

When a question asks where predictive and generative models live, answer AI Models, the former Einstein Studio, in Data 360. When a scenario needs a no-code predictive model built with clicks, answer Model Builder. When a scenario has a model already trained on an external platform, answer Bring Your Own Model, which runs inference against that externally trained model without moving data. When a model's predictions do not appear where expected, answer that the model has not been activated. And when a scenario asks how a score creates value, answer that it must surface somewhere and drive an action, usually through a real-time data graph feeding next best action, next best offer, or Agentforce decisioning. Hold those five rules and this objective is nearly automatic.
