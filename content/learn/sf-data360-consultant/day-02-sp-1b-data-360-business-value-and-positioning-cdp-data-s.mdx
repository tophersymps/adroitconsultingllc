# Lesson 2: Data 360 Business Value and Positioning: CDP, Data Silos, Credits, and Data Cloud One

A client says they already bought a customer data platform, so why would they look at Data 360? The consultant who answers with a feature list loses the room. The consultant who answers with the real question, which is why their sales, service, and marketing teams still argue about whose number is right, wins it. The business case for Data 360 is not that it is a better CDP in the abstract. It is that it turns fragmented, siloed data into one trusted, real time view of the customer and then acts on it inside the tools those teams already use.

That framing is testable, and the exam tests it. This lesson covers requirement sp-1b, part of the Solution Positioning domain worth 14% of the Salesforce Certified Data 360 Consultant exam. The requirement asks you to describe Data 360's key terminology and business value, covering the business case a consultant must make: data silos, the Salesforce-native CDP position, credits and consumption, and the Data Cloud One multi-org model. Those are four arguments you will make over and over, so this lesson builds each one to be reused in a real meeting, not just recognized on a question.

## Deep Dive

### The business value is one trusted view that acts inside the tools teams already use

The value of Data 360 is that it turns fragmented, siloed data into one trusted, real time view of the customer, and then acts on that view inside the tools teams already use.[^1] It is a cloud native, metadata driven platform deeply integrated with the Salesforce metadata framework, and that integration is the difference that matters.[^3] Because external data is mapped to the Customer 360 Data Model and behaves like standard Salesforce objects and fields, the platform does not treat outside data as a second class citizen that lives somewhere off to the side. It becomes first class data that any Salesforce feature can read.

Think about what that replaces. Without a shared foundation, sales, service, marketing, commerce, and AI agents each stitch together their own brittle point to point integrations, and every new use case adds another connection to maintain. With Data 360 as the foundation, those teams share one governed data layer, and an agent that needs customer context reads the same unified profile that the service rep sees. That is why the platform is positioned as the data foundation rather than a side project owned by one department.

The silo problem is the business case. Data warehouses and lakehouses are valuable, but they serve as data silos in the sense that the data cannot be acted on directly inside the business flows and applications where the work happens, and they lack the real time and generative AI processing that modern experiences demand.[^3] Data 360 closes that gap by making enterprise data actionable in the flow of work, which is the sentence to lead with when a client says they already have a warehouse.

### Position it as the Salesforce-native CDP that is more than a marketing CDP

Data 360 is the Salesforce native customer data platform, and the positioning decision is whether you let a client file it under marketing or under the whole business. Traditional CDPs store and route customer data for marketing teams. Data 360 does that job and then powers sales, service, commerce, analytics, and Agentforce as well, with identity resolution across devices, households, and touchpoints.[^2] The exam wants the broad framing: Data 360 is the Salesforce-native data platform that unifies data and powers AI across sales, service, marketing, and commerce.

When a client says they already have a CDP and asks why Data 360, the honest answer is native CRM integration, real time action, and one data foundation for every team and every agent, not another isolated marketing tool. The point is not that the incumbent CDP is bad. It is that a marketing only tool cannot give a service agent a unified view or give an AI agent trusted grounding, and it cannot reach into sales and commerce the way a platform that lives inside Salesforce can. The strongest distractors on the exam limit Data 360 to marketing for exactly this reason, because that limit is the positioning mistake the requirement is written to catch.

Salesforce itself frames this carefully. Data 360 is described as more than a CDP because it collects, unifies, and activates customer data into a persistent Customer 360 profile, and then extends beyond traditional CDPs by powering real time data and AI driven insights across sales, service, and commerce, not just marketing.[^2] Reuse that sentence shape with clients: capable of the CDP job, positioned against a narrower category.

### Zero Copy changes the cost and governance story

Zero Copy is the capability that makes the reach argument affordable. Instead of extracting and loading warehouse or lake data into Salesforce, Data 360 federates and shares it in place, so storage and pipeline costs drop and lineage stays clear.[^1] It supports structured and unstructured sources, and the Zero Copy partner network spans providers such as Snowflake, Databricks, Google, AWS, and Microsoft. The consultant framing is simple: reach all enterprise data for AI and analytics without paying to duplicate it and without weakening governance.

The governance half of that sentence is the part clients remember. When you copy data into a new system, you create a second place where the data must be secured, classified, and kept current, and every copy is a new risk surface. Zero Copy avoids that by leaving the data where it lives and accessing it in place. This is also why an answer that requires you to copy all warehouse data into Data 360 is wrong. It misses the entire cost and governance argument, and it describes the old world that Data 360 was designed to move past.

### Consumption is metered, so the business case has to include credits

Data 360 pricing is consumption based, meaning a client spends credits only for the services they use, and you can directly influence the cost by controlling usage.[^4] That flexibility is a selling point, but it puts an obligation on the consultant to estimate consumption honestly. When a service runs, it generates a usage event, and each usage event has a usage type; credits are deducted by usage type as work runs. The sentence the exam is looking for is exact: Data 360 is consumption-based: actions generate usage events that consume credits by usage type.

There are two credit currencies, and a consultant should know which one their client holds. Data Services credits are unique to Data 360 and can only power Data 360 services, and they are split into many usage types with different rates.[^4] Flex Credits are interchangeable across Data 360, Agentforce, and other features, and they include their own Data 360 usage types alongside usage types for generative AI and LLM prompts. The pricing mechanics reward volume: Flex Credit usage types are charged at a specific rate for the first tier of usage each month, and additional usage in any usage type is automatically discounted, with the tiers resetting at the start of every month.[^4]

The reason this belongs in a positioning lesson is that the design decision that drives value is also the decision that drives cost. A use case that unions large datasets repeatedly without a proven outcome burns credits, and features often consume more than one usage type. Previewing a batch transform, for example, consumes a query usage type in addition to the transform usage type you would expect.[^4] Estimate at the usage type level, group the estimate by the connect, harmonize, and unify work versus the analyze, segment, and activate work, and you will not be surprised by the bill.[^5]

### Licensing decides what the client can do before a single stream is configured

Licensing determines the boundaries of the solution, and the exam checks the details. Data 360 is available in Developer, Enterprise, Performance, and Unlimited editions and in Government Cloud.[^6] It runs only in Lightning Experience. The product license turns Data 360 on, and the key rule to remember is that only one Data 360 license of any kind can sit in an org. That single rule is what makes the multi-org conversation unavoidable for large clients, because you cannot simply buy Data 360 five times for five orgs.[^7]

Two SKUs come up in the field. The Data 360 Starter SKU is the paid path for customers who have Data 360 use cases, and it carries the larger credit allotment and storage allocation. The Data 360 Provisioning SKU, sometimes called the Everywhere SKU, is a zero cost SKU with fewer credits for customers who do not have Data 360 use cases but are using other Salesforce products that rely on Data 360 functionality.[^7] Both turn on the platform, and the difference is capacity and cost rather than capability, so a consultant should describe the choice as an entitlement decision, not a feature decision.

Add on licenses extend the base. Data Spaces, Data Masking, Platform Encryption for Data Cloud, Sub-Second Real-Time Profile and Entities, and storage beyond allocation are all add ons, and the Data Cloud One Companion Org license is what lets an org act as a companion rather than a home org.[^6] A question that asks which license an org needs for a specific capability, or whether an org can hold two Data 360 licenses, is testing this section. The answers are short: pick the add on for the capability, and remember that an org holds one Data 360 license of any kind.

### Data Cloud One is the multi-org architecture

Data Cloud One is the multi-org connectivity model, and it is the answer whenever a scenario mentions more than one org. One Salesforce org is the home org that hosts the single Data 360 instance, and it is the central hub where data is ingested and stored and where the unified Customer 360 view lives.[^8] Other orgs connect as companion orgs through a companion connection, a bridge that shares metadata for the data spaces you choose to share, and they consume unified data and metadata as if it were local.[^8]

All ingestion configuration happens from the home org, and the home org runs identity resolution, harmonization, modeling, and unification. Companion orgs get access to a subset of Data 360 functionality through the Data Cloud One app, and Data 360 data becomes available there to power platform features such as Flow and enrichments.[^8] Choosing the home org is a long term architectural and regional decision, because the unified data layer is centralized, so a client with data residency requirements may need multiple regional clusters of Data Cloud One rather than one global home org.[^3]

When an enterprise has five Salesforce orgs and wants one governed Data 360 instance, the recommended pattern is to use Data Cloud One with one home org hosting Data 360 and the other orgs as companion orgs. Salesforce recommends Data Cloud One over licensing Data 360 in each org, and the reason is both cost and governance: licensing each org multiplies spend and fragments governance, while a companion arrangement keeps one source of truth and shares only the metadata you select.[^7]

### Writing the positioning one liner

The walkthrough you can run with a client, or trace in a trial org, turns the four arguments into one recommendation.

1. In the Your Account app or Setup, review which Data 360 license is provisioned and the entitlements it carries, such as credits, storage, default data spaces, and companion org connections.
2. Open the Data 360 credit consumption dashboards in Digital Wallet and identify the usage types that dominate current or planned consumption.
3. Map a proposed use case to its dominant usage types, for example unification, activation, or query, so the business case can estimate credit cost.
4. Compare licensing a Data 360 instance per org against the Data Cloud One pattern of one home org plus companion orgs, and note the regional implication of the home org choice.
5. Write the positioning one liner: unify all enterprise data, act on it inside Salesforce, and ground AI on it, with Zero Copy avoiding duplication.

That last step is the deliverable. A positioning one liner that names unification, action inside Salesforce, AI grounding, and Zero Copy is one a client can repeat to their own executives, and a consultant whose recommendation survives a retelling has done the job.

## Exam Traps

Three traps dominate this objective, and each one is a positioning mistake dressed up as a fact.

First, assuming Data 360 is only for marketing or is a plain CDP. It powers sales, service, commerce, analytics, and Agentforce, so a marketing only framing undersells the platform and is the wrong positioning answer. When you see an option that limits Data 360 to marketing, treat it as the trap.

Second, assuming you must copy all warehouse data into Data 360. Zero Copy federation and sharing access the data where it lives, so any answer that requires a full physical copy misses the cost and governance argument. Copying everything is the old integration model, not the Data 360 model.

Third, assuming credits are unlimited or that a poorly designed use case costs nothing. Consumption is metered per usage type, so a design that unions large datasets repeatedly without a proven outcome burns credits. If a scenario ignores cost, the right instinct is to surface the usage types and estimate, not to wave the cost away.

## What a consultant should walk away with

Lead with the business outcome, not the feature list: one trusted, real time view of the customer, acted on inside the tools teams already use. Position Data 360 as the Salesforce native data platform that spans every function and grounds AI, never as a marketing only tool. Reach for Zero Copy whenever the data already lives in a lake or warehouse, because reaching it in place is the cost and governance argument. Treat consumption as part of the design, estimating at the usage type level and remembering that Flex Credits reset and discount monthly while Data Services credits are Data 360 only. And the moment a scenario mentions more than one org, answer with Data Cloud One: one home org, companion orgs, one governed instance.
